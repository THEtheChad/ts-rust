# D0-1A: does output depend on the checker partition? (2026-11-04)

Lane D0-1A (Haiku 5.5). Driver: `scripts/perf-rearch/d0/` (`b950fd9e6`). Raw
data: `~/.cache/perf-rearch/scratch/d0/full/report.json`.

- **Bin:** a stable build at `fc08b5f43` (perf branch with E0).
- **Settings:** `--checkers 1/2/4/8/16`, `--fast`, `--fast` with
  `GOPORT_FAST_STEAL=1`, and `--fast` with lazy members and never-order off.
  3 runs each, compared with the default (4 checkers).
- **Inputs:** 10 projects (22 configs × noemit/emit/extdiag), plus 1,719
  multi-file corpus cases. 42,840 runs, with no crash or timeout.

## Results

- **Output (stdout, exit, stderr, emitted files) depends on the partition in
  1 of 10 projects:** zod's `zod` config, in 3 of its 66 items. It depends on
  it in **0 of 1,719** corpus cases (273 stop before checking).
- **Zod at default:** TS5115 ×6 and TS2344 ×1, which matches Go exactly.
  **Every other setting differs** (TS5115 from 2 to 6, TS2344 from 0 to 2).
  TS5115 is a limit code; TS2344 follows it, but not always in step (at c8
  TS5115 is unchanged and TS2344 gains one). No cycle codes or tie-break
  differences appear anywhere.
- **Printed counts** (Types, Symbols, Instantiations) differ in most
  configs at any non-default setting: c1 down to −75% types, c16 up to
  +300%. With lazy members and never-order off, `--fast` leaves several
  counts at default, so those switches drive most of fast's count changes.
- **Nondeterminism:** none at any fixed partition. **Work stealing is not
  deterministic:** 19 of 66 items vary in counts between runs, and zod's
  TS5115 varies between 5 and 6.

## Implications for D1 (certified partition-free checking)

- A partition-free default must either reproduce Go's exact split on zod-like
  projects or fall back to it. So the monitor has to fire on every zod run;
  experiment 1B tests that.
- Printed counts always force exact mode.
- Stealing would need the monitor to fire on every nondeterministic run, or be
  kept out of certified mode.
- The corpus cases are small and say little about large programs.
  Partition-sensitive cases need targeted, adversarial fixtures (Astra
  finding 8).
